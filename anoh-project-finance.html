A $750m gas plant that cannot yet service its own debt

ANOH reached first gas in January 2026, four years behind its original guidance. I built a full project finance model of the joint venture to answer one question: how much gas has to flow before the facility covers the debt raised to build it?

The asset

ANOH Gas Processing Company is an incorporated joint venture owned equally by Seplat Energy and the Nigerian Gas Infrastructure Company. It sits on OML 53 in Imo State and is the first of seven gas development projects identified by the Federal Government to enter operation. The plant itself was delivered well: 17.8 million man-hours without a lost-time incident, zero routine flaring by design. The difficulty is not the engineering.

Why the gas is not flowing

The plant is mechanically complete and commercially live. Its constraint is evacuation. Three routes were meant to carry 300 MMscfd to market and only one of them is open.

Indorama petrochemical plant

Flowing since 16 January 2026

Firm and interruptible gas sales agreements, reached through an 11 km dedicated export pipeline completed just before first gas. This is the only route currently taking molecules, and it is where the entire 50–70 MMscfd is going.

Nigeria LNG

Mechanically ready, awaiting clearance

All mechanical, commercial and regulatory requirements are complete. The offtake is structured on an interruptible basis, which means it adds volume but not the contracted certainty a lender would price.

OB3 pipeline

Under construction, no completion date announced

Originally the primary channel to the domestic market and the single largest block of assumed offtake. Construction has resumed under a revised execution strategy by its owner, but no revised completion date has been published. The debt case was built on a pipeline that AGPC does not own and cannot schedule.

What the four-year delay cost

In February 2021 the project was announced as fully funded at $680m against a Q4 2021 first-gas plan. It reached first gas 4.05 years later having drawn $750m. The difference is not only the extra $70m.

What actually drives cover

Flexing each driver by 25% around the steady-state case gives a clear ranking, and it is not the one most commentary would predict. The gas price sits fifth. The structural finding

Seplat earns from ANOH twice. It receives dividends on its 50% equity stake, and it sells wet gas into the plant from its 40% working interest in OML 53. That second stream is an operating cost of the project company, so it is paid before the senior lenders are served.

The sponsor ranks ahead of the lenders in the project's own cash waterfall.

This is not unusual in an integrated gas chain, but it changes how the credit should be read. Seplat's exposure to a slow ramp is materially better protected than AGPC's, and the transfer price that governs the split is set between related parties and is not disclosed. For a lender, that is the term worth negotiating hardest.

How the model was built

AGPC's own accounts are not published, and Seplat equity-accounts its 50% interest, so the joint venture's revenue, operating cost and debt balance are not separately visible in the group financial statements. Every input is therefore classified by its evidential status, and the conclusions are stated as breakevens and sensitivities rather than as forecasts.

Structure

Seventeen tabs, 6,605 formulas, nineteen automated integrity checks. Semi-annual periods from H1 2020 through H2 2037, covering construction, the delay, commercial operations and full debt amortisation. A single scenario switch drives throughput, offtake availability, pricing, currency and interest rates across every downstream schedule.

The three assumptions that carry the conclusions

Wet gas priced at 55% of total product revenue - not disclosed, and the top-ranked sensitivity. Senior debt at SOFR + 600bp amortising over eight years from H2 2026 — the facility's pricing and tenor are not public, and the lenders' waiver deferring principal to 31 March 2026 was very likely accompanied by an unannounced maturity extension. Tax modelled as companies income tax plus tertiary education tax with a three-year gas utilisation holiday - whether AGPC qualifies under the PIA midstream regime is not disclosed.

Condensate yield, and why it survives scrutiny

The model uses 48 barrels per MMscf. Two independent disclosures agree: condensate of 2.0–2.5 kboepd against 40–52 MMscfd of processed gas at start-up, and Seplat's OML 53 working-interest 2P ratio of 52.9 MMbbl of liquids to 851.9 Bscf of gas. Liquids revenue is a large share of the case, so the yield is stressed explicitly rather than assumed away.

What would change the answer

A firm completion date for OB3, a firm rather than interruptible NLNG offtake, or disclosure of the wet gas transfer price. Any one of the three would narrow the uncertainty more than refining the model further would.
